# Policy portfolios

**ID:** `design.institutions.policy-portfolios`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.state.agreements-commitments-and-capacity`, `design.institutions.authority-and-action-ownership`

The Chair and represented decision owners manage portfolios of prepared,
conditional, conflicting, complementary, and mutually exclusive plans rather
than isolated event-card responses. A policy package is a persistent record
containing a rationale, constituent actions, conditions, authorities,
dependencies, communications, commitments, monitoring obligations, exit logic,
and alternatives. It belongs to its actual component owner, never a sovereign
or regional composition root.

Package preparation does not authorize or execute its actions. Submission admits a complete proposed slate atomically when its combined constraints are valid. Each constituent action then follows its own decision, authority, execution, take-up, clearing, settlement, and review lifecycle. Failure of one stage does not imply failure or rollback of unrelated committed stages unless an explicit clause couples them.

The same primitive action vocabulary supports monetary policy, liquidity facilities, supervision, communication, coordination, and institutional projects. Player-facing labels and historical strategies compose from these primitives. Packages may reserve concrete staff, meeting, operational, political, credibility, and balance-sheet capacity.

Major resource, transport, water, power, industrial, and security projects use
the same lifecycle within reviewed feasible-project envelopes. Preparation and
finance cannot waive site, technology, permission, capacity, input, or
execution constraints.

A pending proposal may be amended prospectively with linked history. Once an institutional body resolves it, later change uses that body's real legal and procedural lifecycle. Monitoring and postmortem obligations remain attached until satisfied, transferred, cancelled, or released by a witnessed transition.
