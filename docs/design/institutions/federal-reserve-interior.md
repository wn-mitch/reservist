# Federal Reserve interior

**ID:** `design.institutions.federal-reserve-interior`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.representation.people-offices-and-institutions`, `design.institutions.authority-and-action-ownership`

The Federal Reserve is an institutional system, not one Agent. The Board, FOMC, Reserve Banks, offices, staff units, legal functions, supervision, operating desks, facilities, accounts, procedures, and records compose through explicit identity, ownership, authority, access, and execution contracts.

The Chair controls agenda, proposals, convening, delegation, negotiation, and privileged signaling within current authority. Governors and Reserve Bank presidents retain independent judgment and votes. Staff units produce sourced work rather than a single institutional belief. Legal determines its own advice. The FOMC and Board authorize through distinct procedures. The New York Fed and other responsible owners execute only valid instructions and retain operational state.

Default to the parent institution unless a subobject independently owns a consequential queue, account, authority, access boundary, commitment, condition, facility ledger, or execution result. A promoted desk or facility has stable identity but receives no unlisted legal or accounting ownership.

Shared records and official commitments persist across officeholders. Private cognition and personal relationships remain with their people. System-level projections may aggregate the interior for presentation, but they cannot choose or execute on its behalf.
