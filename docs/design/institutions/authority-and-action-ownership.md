# Authority and action ownership

**ID:** `design.institutions.authority-and-action-ownership`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.institutions.legal-regime`, `design.state.causal-and-stage-contracts`

Every consequential path declares distinct owners for proposal, deliberation, authorization, instruction, execution, accounting, settlement, observation, and review. No actor obtains another stage merely by participating in the first. The Chair may propose; the FOMC or Board may authorize; an operating institution may execute; a counterparty may accept; a market may clear; account owners may settle.

The same separation governs foreign resource systems. Legislatures, ministries,
regulators, state firms, private producers, infrastructure operators, central
banks, sovereign funds, security forces, carriers, insurers, banks, and buyers
retain their own stages. Licensing, taxation, quotas, domestic reservation,
administered pricing, rationing, export controls, route allocation, sanctions,
security policy, receipt conversion, reserve use, and portfolio changes occur
only through the competent period owner.

A decision context composes the current person, office, institution, applicable body or authority, available information, goals, plans, relationships, and constraints. There is no universal Actor type that collapses those sources. Role capability exposes candidate actions. Effective law and procedure decide permission. Responsible systems perform execution. Action results answer requesters; domain events and transactions prove effects.

Decision bodies retain participant rosters, voting or consensus rules, quorum, agenda, eligible motions, and results. Their outcome is not the Chair's personal action. Staff recommendations do not authorize. Institutional ownership does not imply that every employee can act. Informal influence is modeled as an action with cost, resistance, and exposure, never as authority.

An announcement may alter beliefs without changing law; enacted authority may
fail to produce an instruction; an operator or counterparty may resist or lack
capacity; execution, delivery, or settlement may still fail. Informal coercion
can change another actor's decision through a witnessed action but never
transfers that actor's authority.

Every denied, delayed, failed, or successful stage remains witnessed. Presentation must not summarize a proposal as policy until the authoritative stage actually occurred.
