# Legal regime

**ID:** `design.institutions.legal-regime`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.representation.relationships-and-affiliations`, `design.state.actions-results-and-events`

Canonical law is a set of effective `LegalInstrument` identities and typed clauses. Clauses define powers, duties, prohibitions, eligibility, delegations, designations, procedures, effective dates, compliance periods, enforcement, supersession, and repeal. Formal authority comes only from effective law or a valid delegation. Informal pressure, custom, urgency, and political leverage may affect behavior but cannot manufacture legal power.

Congress and other competent authorities independently own legislation, oversight, appointments, and legal transitions. The Chair may advise, testify, coordinate, negotiate, and apply institutional pressure but is not a parliamentary whip. A designation is clause or eligibility state, not a new representation kind.

Enactment, amendment, delegated rulemaking, effectiveness, compliance, enforcement, suspension, supersession, and repeal are separate dated transitions. A historical enactment may schedule future effectiveness; a later valid action may alter the prospective path without erasing history.

Legal staff hold fallible interpretations based on accessible sources. An incorrect opinion changes institutional belief and may affect action, exposure, or delay, but never rewrites canonical law. Invalid or unauthorized action attempts remain witnessed facts and can create consequences without becoming legally effective.
