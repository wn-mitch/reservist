# Relationships and affiliations

**ID:** `design.representation.relationships-and-affiliations`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.state.agreements-commitments-and-capacity`

Roles, contracts, memberships, accounts, and informal relationships are separate semantic families. They share stable identity, typed endpoints, effective period, provenance, observability, lifecycle, and disappearance behavior, but each family defines its own authority, access, ownership, obligation, and exit semantics. A common edge structure must not erase those differences.

Structural relationships connect scopes, owners, processes, products, contracts, and markets. Every relationship explicitly states whether it conveys authority, access, eligibility, an obligation, title, control, or no ownership effect. The default is no ownership effect. Composition and affiliation do not transmit causal effects; cross-owner effects use typed transmissions.

Officeholding grants only the effective office's legal and delegated capabilities. Employment does not imply institutional authority. Ownership does not automatically confer operational control. Customer, depositor, borrower, investor, student, voter, association, treaty, and correspondent relationships retain distinct exit and information consequences.

Relationships may change during play through witnessed formation, amendment, suspension, expiry, transfer, or termination. Renaming or presentation changes do not replace the relationship or its subjects. Derived networks may index relationships for search and routing but never become canonical owners.
