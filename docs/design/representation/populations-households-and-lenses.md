# Populations, households, and lenses

**ID:** `design.representation.populations-households-and-lenses`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.representation.representation-axes`

People are the conserved population base. Canonical state uses scenario-bounded sparse joint person cells for intersections that own stocks, constrain transactions, or materially change a transmission channel. Conditional distributions retain lower-priority and continuous variation. Cells may split, merge, appear, and disappear only through witnessed demographic, geographic, educational, employment, and household transitions while total people reconcile.

Every person belongs to one current household arrangement. Household cohorts may own shared income, consumption, debt service, housing, care obligations, and financial stocks while preserving protected member correlations. Household formation, dissolution, and membership are explicit events rather than generic tag changes.

Pops, constituencies, audiences, and identities are non-owning lenses over canonical person and household state. They may select intersections and derive exposure, belief distributions, behavior, participation, political influence, and media reach. A lens never owns headcount, votes, labor, deposits, debt, or consumption. Cross-base lenses declare a common unit and may aggregate compatible stocks, never incompatible counts.

Population scale creates separate demographic, consumption, productive, financial, electoral, organizational, elite-access, disruptive, and narrative capacities. Distributed response systems realize bounded behavior; Pops do not become collective Agents without a coordinating organization.
