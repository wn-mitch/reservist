# Sovereign necessity systems

**ID:** `design.representation.sovereign-necessity-systems`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.representation.coalitions-sovereigns-and-geography`, `design.economy.real-economy-network`, `design.information.observation-evidence-and-belief`

A coarse sovereign bundle assigns each facet to exactly one typed component
owner beneath the identity root. The institutional facets are governing
authority, fiscal accounts, monetary accounts, external relationships,
aggregate trade production, aggregate trade demand, and bounded response
tables. The necessity facets are water and sanitation, food, energy, shelter
and thermal needs, health, transport and logistics, payments and finance,
communications, public order and security, and strategic materiel.

Every sovereign carries every necessity facet. A facet may be declared not
applicable only with a cited reason. In a federation, the facet stays under the
federal root and its owner may be a cohort of sub-national owners; states,
provinces, and emirates do not become roots by owning a system.

Resource systems such as petroleum, gas, or grain are extensions a sovereign
declares, not universal facets. Each declared resource system names distinct
policy-authority and operations owners under the resource-system rule of the
real-economy network. Aggregate trade production and demand cover flows that no
necessity facet or resource extension already owns.

Each necessity system preserves the causal stages from capacity or stock to
deliverable availability to realized access. These need not be literal fields,
but no model may collapse them into one supply scalar. Transport, payment,
distribution, pricing, legal restriction, rationing, and regional disruption
can reduce access without destroying stock or productive capacity. An access
shock never mutates upstream production, inventory, or capacity state.

A system carries only the stocks, capacities, constraints, failures,
dependencies, and observations needed to explain whether governments and
institutions can act. Cross-system dependencies are explicit: a payments
failure reaches food access through a declared transmission, not a shared
meter. Household routing, detailed production scheduling, and supply-chain
optimization are outside the model and never become player tasks.

Coarse fidelity may compress a system's state and behavior. It may not merge
facet owners, because each facet varies independently, holds different
canonical state, and answers to different authority. Calibration of stocks,
capacities, demand, access, and responses is authored content keyed by
sovereign and facet, never a constant inside a model family.
