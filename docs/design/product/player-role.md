# Player role

**ID:** `design.product.player-role`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.product.premise`

The player controls the current Chair's attributable choices, not the Federal Reserve as a single actor. The Chair can set agendas, request and prioritize work, convene people, propose packages, negotiate, communicate, delegate, and apply lawful or informal institutional pressure. Other officeholders, governors, Reserve Bank presidents, staff, legal counsel, supervisors, operators, counterparties, markets, and political authorities retain their own roles, beliefs, procedures, and constraints.

The player cannot command votes, manufacture legal authority, guarantee operational execution, compel facility take-up, choose market interpretation, inspect private beliefs, or read canonical world state. Informal influence is a costly action that may persuade, fail, provoke resistance, or create legal and reputational exposure; it never becomes formal authority.

Player decisions occur through finite institutional contexts and explicit handoff. Inspection and deliberation are reversible until an effectful slate is submitted, except for authored speech acts that explicitly commit when spoken. Consequences continue through authorization, execution, settlement, observation, and later review. Failure usually changes the institutional environment and future action set before it ends play.
