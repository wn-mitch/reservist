# Media circuit

**ID:** `design.information.media-circuit`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.information.claims-publication-and-audiences`, `design.representation.generators-processes-and-references`

Media composes people, institutions, outlets, networks, records, claims, and audiences rather than introducing a universal pundit or debate kind. Recurring commentators use named cognition only when their stable beliefs, relationships, reputation, affiliations, and discretionary communication materially affect distribution. Other voices use limited role-holder or cohort response.

An outlet owns editorial slate, publication identity, archive, and released records. A network owns distribution relationships and reach. The operating company, editor, personality, and owner remain separate represented subjects when their action sets matter. None receives canonical economic truth.

Period media may include newspapers, broadcasts, wires, market commentary,
diplomatic traffic, intelligence, and staff assessment. Their access,
selection, interpretation, incentives, release timing, and revision practices
remain persistent source properties rather than one generic reliability score.
Foreign developments can remain in the ordinary information environment before
any participant understands their economic importance.

Appearances, interviews, panels, debates, editorials, leaks, and corrections resolve as bounded sequences of authored communication choices and publication stages. Claims can be challenged, reframed, repeated, verified, ignored, or corrected. Every stage has a source and witness. The same evidence may reach audiences at different times and credibility, producing divergent beliefs and behavior.

Satirical doctrine personae may exist as authored presentation or records, not as supernatural canonical agents. Media attention can create player-facing opportunities when institutionally routed, but ambient coverage need not become a mandatory appointment.
