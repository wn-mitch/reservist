# Claims, publication, and audiences

**ID:** `design.information.claims-publication-and-audiences`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.information.observation-evidence-and-belief`

Causal communication uses structured claims rather than arbitrary prose. A claim records subject, predicate, magnitude, horizon, modality, conditions, confidence, venue, intended audience, evidence, omissions, coordination status, speaker, and timing. Durable communication acts, reports, transcripts, and assessments are records that reference claims and their evidence provenance.

Publication is a witnessed transition that changes exposure before behavior. It identifies the publishing owner, outlet or channel, released claims, framing, corrections, audience reach, access conditions, and time. Audiences then receive different subsets and framings according to their media diets, relationships, access, repetition, and source credibility. Receipt may update beliefs and later behavior; publication never writes beliefs directly.

Authored prose renders reviewed claims for context. The renderer cannot invent commitments, soften a legal effect, add a causal proposition, or assign an outcome. Player speech binds the selected authored line to typed claims and actions before publication.

Corrections and retractions are new records linked to the original release. They do not erase prior exposure. Audience-specific belief changes retain which version arrived, when it arrived, and through which source.
