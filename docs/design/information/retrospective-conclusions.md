# Retrospective conclusions

**ID:** `design.information.retrospective-conclusions`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.information.observation-evidence-and-belief`, `design.state.correction-and-invalidation`

In-world postmortems are ordinary institutionally sourced records, never omniscient explanations. Each conclusion distinguishes evidence known at decision time, accessible but missed, unavailable then, learned later, and still inaccessible or disputed. It names author, supporting and contrary evidence, confidence, dissent, timing, and relevant access limits.

Later evidence may revise an assessment but must be labeled as hindsight. A supplemental or amended review links to the earlier record and preserves what it originally concluded. Disputed attribution remains a finding with confidence and dissent; it does not become canonical certainty merely because a review states it.

The player may accept the record, attach a Chair response, request a specific evidence-backed revision, or commission a supplemental review under real capacity and timing constraints. Dispositioning a record does not require agreement. Refusing to close an adverse report cannot suppress institutional history or any separately valid evaluative consequence.

Developer traces may inspect complete canonical causality for debugging and validation. Staff reports and player-facing postmortems cannot. Player comprehension is tested by whether the player can explain what was known, what remained uncertain, and which lesson transfers—not by agreement with a preferred policy.
