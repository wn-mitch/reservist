# Access and reporting

**ID:** `design.information.access-and-reporting`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.information.evidence-routing`, `design.institutions.staff-capability-and-condition`

Access controls apply mechanically to every source and recipient. Routine authorized task assignment obtains purpose-bound access through competent institutional workflow without repeated player prompts. Forwarding a task or evidence reference does not broaden the source's scope. Wider distribution requires an authorized grant, sanitization, aggregation, publication, or another witnessed transition.

Nonroutine conflicts surface only when they materially change timing, evidence quality, awareness, confidentiality exposure, staff load, or institutional relationships. Available responses include reassignment, restricted joint work, sanitized summaries, narrower questions, additional authorization, explicit delay, conditional analysis, or proceeding with known gaps.

A hard decision deadline never yields a contentless refusal. Competent staff present multiple institutionally feasible choices and state delivery time, uncertainty, missing evidence, capacity cost, displaced work, and known risk. Preliminary or conditional judgments explain which future evidence would distinguish branches.

Reporting friction usually appears as delay, stale inputs, narrower sources, reduced confidence, or consumed capacity. It is not a generic approval minigame. Meeting access remains contextual: brought artifacts, current materials, notes, and participants are immediate; retrieval outside that context follows real access and time constraints.
