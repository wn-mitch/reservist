# Calendar and interruptions

**ID:** `design.interface.calendar-and-interruptions`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.runtime.ordering-and-temporal-cadence`, `design.institutions.chief-of-staff`

The calendar board is the primary action surface. It shows mandatory institutional anchors, deadlines, scheduled processes, current commitments, expected deliveries, discretionary activity periods, and known interruption conditions. The chief supplies a competent default agenda. The player may inspect, reprioritize, delegate, or replace discretionary work while fixed obligations and real capacity remain visible.

Advancement is a discrete player-authorized action and never tracks wall time. Dates may pass during resolution without empty turns. Reading delivered artifacts and previewing choices do not advance the calendar. Activities show owner, allocation, start, expected completion or payoff, displaced alternatives, and release conditions.

Observed calls, emergency meetings, deadlines, and committed conditions may interrupt before the next briefing. An interruption banner announces new material without replacing the open folder's settled decision context. The player can inspect the interruption, park the existing folder, and restore it with pencils and source context intact. Speaking commitments and already submitted work remain canonical.

Stop rules use observations, schedules, doctrine, and commitments rather than hidden crisis significance. Accumulated briefings preserve trends so skipped uneventful dates do not make conditions jump without an institutional evidence trail.
