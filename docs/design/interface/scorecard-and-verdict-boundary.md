# Scorecard and verdict boundary

**ID:** `design.interface.scorecard-and-verdict-boundary`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.campaign.stewardship-score`, `design.information.retrospective-conclusions`

The in-world institutional review and the extradiegetic Stewardship score are separate surfaces. Staff postmortems remain fallible records with no universal verdict. They explain evidence, reasoning, dissent, and institutional consequences within their access. The scorecard gives the player a deliberately reductive headline evaluation without entering world state.

When a completed review is dispositioned, the scorecard may present an authored verdict and signed delta first, followed by an inspectable finding ledger. Each finding names visible criteria, witnessed evidence, magnitude, and the accepted review version. The display cannot reveal an undiscovered subject; contingent criteria appear only when their subject becomes institutionally known.

Accepting a review means dispositioning its record, not agreeing with it. A Chair response, requested evidence-backed revision, or supplemental review remains visible beside the score entry. Refusing closure cannot suppress an adverse valid delta. Later evidence produces an explicit compensating adjustment rather than rewriting the prior score or report.

World actors never observe the score. The interface never presents it as credibility, legitimacy, authority, reputation, or causal capacity.
