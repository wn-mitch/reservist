# Canonical state and ownership

**ID:** `design.state.canonical-state-and-ownership`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `none`

Canonical state contains facts whose change alters history: identities, effective relationships, accounts, stocks, conditions, legal authority, obligations, beliefs, plans, queues, records, commitments, execution results, and witnessed transitions. Every canonical value has one declared owner. Other systems may reference, observe, aggregate, or project it but may not maintain competing truth.

Derived caches, indexes, dashboards, situation views, staff products, Pop lenses, coalition options, score projections, and player-safe views remain non-owning. A cache cannot authorize an action, settle a transaction, survive as an independent source of truth, or repair missing canonical state. Rebuilding a derived value from persisted owners must produce the same result.

Identity clade, canonical owner class, cognition, authority, selected fidelity, and presentation salience are separate axes. Stable identity does not imply ownership, legal personality, a mind, authority, or rich simulation. Cross-owner effects use declared typed transitions and witnesses. Conserved quantities move only through balanced owner-specific entries; non-conserved conditions still require a witnessed owner transition.

One real-world subject may compose several owners without duplication: a person, office, institution, decision body, account, facility, and record can cooperate while retaining separate state and lifecycle boundaries.
