# Cognition, beliefs, and plans

**ID:** `design.state.cognition-beliefs-and-plans`
**Status:** `canonical`
**Depends on:** `design.state.canonical-state-and-ownership`

Persistent cognition consists of beliefs, goals, memory, and plans owned by representations permitted to deliberate. Beliefs are typed estimates with structural priors, uncertainty, confidence, provenance, update time, and an owner-specific observation model. They update from delivered evidence, never direct reads of canonical truth. Missing evidence may weaken confidence or pull an estimate toward its prior; it does not reveal the missing fact.

Plans persist independently of action selection. New observations can change goal priority, plan achievability, eligibility thresholds, or utility considerations; a plan may continue, revise, or be abandoned according to its commitment strategy. Utility evaluation occurs only when an active plan reaches a consequential, attributable external choice. It selects among actions already exposed by role capability and current context.

A researched strategic interpretation belongs to the component actor that can
deliberate: an officeholder, represented participant, decision body through its
procedure, or another permitted role holder. It may contain authored interior
concerns, beliefs about counterparties and commitments, a bounded posture
vocabulary, and plans whose eligible actions remain constrained by role,
authority, and material state. A `SovereignSystem` never owns this cognition.

Commitment tests deliver typed evidence naming the promisor, promise, stakes,
observed response, material consequence, ambiguity, and information available
to the actor. Revision remains bounded by authored priors and postures.
Uncertainty, incidents, and player action may change evidence or feasible
outcomes; randomness cannot directly rewrite interests or relationships.

Institutions do not have one humanlike mind. People deliberate; represented participants carry beliefs; staff records synthesize evidence and judgment; decision bodies authorize through procedure; institutions preserve durable state and execute through responsible systems. Pops use distributed response rather than collective cognition.

An attributed model may estimate another subject's beliefs, goals, posture, or reaction function. It is fallible, source-bounded, resolution-dependent, and cannot recursively contain attributed models or become the target's actual cognition.
