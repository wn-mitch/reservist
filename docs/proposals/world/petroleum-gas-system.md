# Gulf petroleum and gas system proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.petroleum-gas-system`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.commodity-production-model`, `proposal.world.regional-composition-boundary`, `proposal.world.title-custody-and-transformation`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.economy.real-economy-network`, `design.content.instrument-and-product-families`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

The regional energy composition would distinguish physical stages that create
different decisions or failure modes. Crude would pass from a reservoir process
through extraction, field gathering and treatment, storage, pipeline or coastal
movement, terminal nomination, loading, carriage, discharge, and refinery
intake. Refined products would arise only through a refinery transformation
with explicit feedstock, capacity, yield, losses, grade, storage, and title.
Natural gas would distinguish associated and non-associated supply, processing,
domestic pipeline delivery, liquefaction, LNG storage and loading, carriage,
regasification, and downstream use.

Associated gas would be a witnessed joint output of oil extraction, not a free
substitute for independently produced gas. A crude production cut could
therefore tighten gas available to power, water, petrochemicals, or reinjection;
a gas constraint could in turn limit crude recovery or domestic electricity.
Period content would decide whether flaring, reinjection, sulfur, condensate,
natural-gas liquids, and petrochemical feedstocks merit explicit products or
remain balanced co-product residuals.

The initial product family would explicitly retain crude-quality and sulfur
families, condensate and natural-gas liquids, pipeline gas, LNG, gasoline,
diesel or gasoil, jet fuel, fuel oil, and naphtha. Naphtha, ethane, and LPG could
feed crackers that yield bounded petrochemical intermediates and polymer
families. Compatibility and yield would be promoted where one barrel or cargo
cannot substitute for another; individual commercial grades and polymers could
remain residual unless they create a selected bottleneck.

Each producer would hold a dated operating plan rather than a single supply
curve. The plan would allocate technically available capacity among domestic
delivery, refinery feed, contractual exports, discretionary exports, storage,
maintenance, and shut-in. Execution would then apply field condition, labor and
service inputs, gas and power availability, legal quotas, storage headroom,
pipeline and terminal admission, weather, damage, and restart delay. Spare
capacity would be reported by quantity and earliest sustainable delivery window;
capacity unreachable before the scenario horizon would not answer a current
shortage.

Country-product distinctions would be promoted only when they affect acceptance
or substitution: crude grade and sulfur, refinery configuration, LNG versus
pipeline gas, destination restrictions, sanction status, and contract
flexibility. A benchmark price would never stand in for a delivered cargo. Each
cargo would preserve origin, grade, title account, custody, delivery window,
route, destination eligibility, payment currency, contract, and provenance.

Saudi Arabia would test planned versus realized output, east-west allocation,
domestic crude burn, refining, and associated gas. Qatar would test LNG trains,
long-term customer allocation, shipping, and liquefaction restart. The UAE would
test Abu Dhabi production against Gulf and Fujairah outlets. Iran would test
sanctions, storage, discounted customers, and shut-in risk. Iraq would test
southern gathering and terminal dependence. Kuwait, Oman, and Bahrain would
enter where production, grade, refining, or route capacity replaces a material
residual share.

The source-backed content pass would begin with the
[EIA country analyses](https://www.eia.gov/international/analysis) and operator
reports such as [Saudi Aramco's publications](https://www.aramco.com/en/investors/annual-report),
then reconcile capacities and flows by historical period.
