# Gulf period composition matrix proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.period-composition-matrix`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.regional-composition-boundary`, `proposal.world.observations-and-fed-transmission`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.campaign.scenario-and-campaign-composition`, `design.content.regional-promotion`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

The common regional machinery would support three sibling compositions. Each
would select the few foreign owners whose choices or constraints alter the
Federal Reserve problem; all other detail would remain inside period-specific
residual providers.

| Lens | Promoted regional core | Authored exigencies | Economic question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paul Vulture, 1979–1981 | Iran and Iraq production; Saudi replacement capacity; selected OPEC allocation; tanker delivery; U.S. importers and inventories | Iranian Revolution, subsequent disruption, Iran–Iraq war path | Does a volatile quantity loss become persistent inflation through expectations, inventory, contracts, and weak credibility? |
| Alan Greenspaniel, 1990–1991 | Iraq and Kuwait removal; Saudi and selected Gulf replacement; coalition-route security; strategic stocks; refiners and credit-sensitive importers | invasion, coalition and war calendar, territorial outcome | Is the shock short-lived and reversible, or does uncertainty compound recession, confidence, and financial weakness? |
| Kevin Boarsh, contemporary | Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Iran, Oman, and southern Iraq flows; Hormuz and bypass routes; LNG; carriers, insurers, storage, sanctions, dollar settlement, and portfolios | bounded Iran/Hormuz conflict and diplomatic event queue | Is lost deliverability physical, contractual, financial, informational, or a compound of all four? |

Each lens would receive its own infrastructure graph, country and operator law,
capacity and inventory ledger, product slate, contracts, currencies, accounts,
buyers, observations, and policy authorities. Later infrastructure, institutions,
financial practices, or data would never be back-ported for convenience.

Within a lens, counterfactual fixtures could vary only declared political
formations: producer coordination, replacement pledges, narrow versus broad
route restriction, coalition credibility, sanctions posture, expected duration,
or negotiated corridor. Those choices would be authored at scenario start or
in a revealed event queue. Simulation would determine execution, congestion,
storage, delivery, prices, receipts, balance-sheet effects, beliefs, and domestic
responses. It would not generate an un-authored interstate war or regime change.

Comparison would hold the relevant U.S. opening macro state fixed across sibling
fixtures while allowing shock size to emerge from the composition. Authors would
record which foreign facts are invariant, which fixture inputs vary, which
owners respond endogenously, and which residuals reconcile. The test is not
whether identical oil-price paths produce identical policy. It is whether the
player can distinguish causal structures that initially look alike and choose a
response robust to the uncertainty actually present.

The first implementation gate would be a no-player-action replay proving that
each rich composition reproduces its lower-fidelity energy, freight, financial,
and observation tapes within documented tolerances. Only then would bounded
foreign responses and alternate fixtures be enabled.
