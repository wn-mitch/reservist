# Gulf fiscal, monetary, and portfolio transmission proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.fiscal-monetary-portfolio-transmission`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.national-resource-management`, `proposal.world.petroleum-gas-system`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.economy.accounting-and-settlement`, `design.institutions.policy-portfolios`, `design.economy.opening-conditions-and-transmission`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

Export revenue would travel through contracts and accounts rather than become a
country cash score. A delivered or contractually payable cargo would generate
receivables, settlement obligations, fees, taxes, royalties, dividends, and
retained earnings according to period ownership. Payment could clear in dollars
or another currency, be delayed, discounted, blocked, escrowed, netted, or
defaulted. Title, delivery, invoicing, payment, and final reserve availability
would remain distinct events.

The minimum sovereign composition would separate the petroleum operator, fiscal
authority, central bank or monetary authority, domestic banking system, and any
selected sovereign investment portfolio. The fiscal authority would own budget
accounts, transfers, procurement, debt, subsidies, and tax or dividend claims.
The central bank would own monetary liabilities, bank settlement, foreign
reserves, exchange-rate operations, and liquidity facilities under period law.
Sovereign funds would own their portfolios and mandates. No one owner could
spend another's assets merely because all are public.

Country institutions would differ by regime rather than by cosmetic labels.
Dollar pegs, managed exchange rates, sanctions, capital controls, fiscal
breakevens, domestic debt markets, foreign-currency borrowing, and bank exposure
would be period parameters and legal constraints. Oil receipts could finance
imports and budgets, accumulate as reserves, enter sovereign portfolios, repay
debt, support banks or the exchange rate, or be retained by an operator. A loss
of deliverable exports could therefore produce different combinations of
fiscal compression, reserve use, asset sales, borrowing, arrears, subsidy
reform, and domestic credit stress.

Foreign-asset behavior would connect the region to U.S. markets without assuming
that every receipt buys Treasuries. Selected institutions would allocate among
deposits, government securities, agency or private instruments, equities,
direct investment, and residual foreign assets subject to mandate, liquidity,
currency, sanctions, custody, and risk. Purchases and sales would enter ordinary
instrument markets or typed external-demand providers. Confidential or
unobserved allocations would remain latent provider state, not player knowledge.

For Federal Reserve gameplay, relevant outputs would include dollar funding
demand, bank claims and payment stress, reserve recycling, U.S. asset demand,
exchange-rate defense, importer financing, and changes in Gulf demand for U.S.
goods and services. These channels could reinforce or offset the inflationary
effect of oil. The Chair could provide domestic or authorized international
liquidity and interpret market evidence, but could not redirect a sovereign
budget or portfolio.

Each period pass would reconcile physical export volumes and contract prices to
operator receipts, government revenue, balance-of-payments entries, central-bank
reserves, portfolio flows, and residual statistical discrepancies.
