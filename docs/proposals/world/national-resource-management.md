# National resource management proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.national-resource-management`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.commodity-production-model`, `proposal.world.arabian-reference-chain`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.institutions.policy-portfolios`, `design.content.regional-promotion`, `design.economy.external-provider-contracts`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

National resource power would emerge from a portfolio of separately owned
rights, institutions, accounts, infrastructure, and actions rather than a
sovereign resource score. A `SovereignSystem` would remain a composition root
under `doc:design.representation.coalitions-sovereigns-and-geography`.
Legislatures, ministries, regulators, state firms, private producers,
infrastructure operators, central banks, sovereign funds, security forces, and
foreign counterparties would retain their own authority and behavior.

Foreign political causation follows the authored-exigency and bounded-response
rules in `doc:design.economy.external-provider-contracts`.

A period composition could select independently varying levers for:

- exploration, licensing, concessions, taxation, royalties, and expropriation;
- production targets, quotas, maintenance, investment, and capacity expansion;
- domestic reservation, administered pricing, subsidy, rationing, and strategic
  stocks;
- refinery, liquefaction, power, water, storage, pipeline, terminal, and port
  allocation;
- export licenses, sanctions compliance, customer allocation, and route choice;
- receipt conversion, fiscal spending, currency defense, bank support, reserve
  accumulation, and sovereign-asset purchases or sales; and
- infrastructure protection, navigation policy, military risk, diplomacy, and
  negotiated coordination.

Each lever would use ordinary policy-package and stage contracts. A political
announcement could alter expectations without changing law; enacted authority
could fail to produce an order; a producer could resist or lack capacity; an
operator could ration access; a carrier, crew, insurer, buyer, or bank could
refuse; physical delivery could fail after title or payment obligations formed.
Informal coercion could change another actor's decision but would never transfer
its authority.

A contemporary Hormuz composition would be the first gameplay lens. It would
separate reservoir capacity, planned production, realized extraction, storage,
pipeline bypass, Gulf terminal access, tanker availability, war-risk coverage,
crew willingness, naval routing, cargo eligibility, and destination acceptance.
Restricted transit could strand titled inventory, fill storage, force upstream
shut-ins, impair fiscal receipts, tighten domestic fuel or water-and-power
allocation, and induce reserve or sovereign-portfolio actions without changing
the underlying resource endowment.

The Fed-facing consequences would enter through existing channel boundaries:
energy and import schedules, freight capacity and delay, market clearing,
measured prices and inflation, foreign financial stress, dollar funding, and
foreign demand for U.S. assets. The Chair would observe partial and sometimes
conflicting shipping, insurance, production, inventory, price, diplomatic, and
financial evidence. The player could respond through lawful Federal Reserve
actions and coordination, but could not command foreign production, navigation,
or security outcomes.

Period content would decide which national levers are actually represented.
Unselected political detail would remain inside typed providers and residuals;
economic importance alone would not justify shallow ministries or firms.

Major infrastructure would develop inside authored feasible-project envelopes.
An envelope would bound site or route, product, available technology, eligible
owners, capacity range, cost and lead time, permissions, and physical
dependencies. Actors could propose, finance, resize, delay, accelerate,
mothball, expand, or cancel a reachable project. They could not construct an
arbitrary route because a price signal made it desirable. A plausible need that
escapes every authored envelope would remain a supported frontier outcome, not
a reason for the runtime to invent geography or technology.
