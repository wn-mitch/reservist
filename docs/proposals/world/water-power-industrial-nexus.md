# Gulf water, power, and industrial nexus proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.water-power-industrial-nexus`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.resource-truth-and-estimates`, `proposal.world.petroleum-gas-system`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.economy.real-economy-network`, `design.content.instrument-and-product-families`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

The region's domestic constraint system would join water, electricity, fuels,
and industry without collapsing them into an energy-demand coefficient. Source
water would distinguish seawater, renewable groundwater, fossil groundwater,
surface water where relevant, and reclaimed wastewater. Physical stages would
include abstraction or intake, pretreatment, desalination or treatment, potable
or industrial storage, distribution, consumption, wastewater collection,
treatment, reuse, and discharge. Quality and delivery eligibility would prevent
one stage from silently satisfying another.

Power supply would distinguish generation capacity, fuel nomination, available
capacity, dispatch, transmission, distribution, reserve margin, load shedding,
and delivered electricity. Period compositions could select gas turbines,
oil-fired generation, cogeneration with thermal desalination, reverse-osmosis
desalination, and later solar or nuclear generation. Technology would determine
which fuel, electricity, steam, water, and outage constraints are coupled; there
would be no timeless Gulf response equation.

Households, hospitals, government services, petroleum operations, refineries,
LNG facilities, petrochemicals, agriculture, and other industry would submit
separate demands only where priority or interruption changes an outcome.
Utilities and competent authorities would own tariffs, subsidies, eligibility,
rationing, emergency reserves, and load-shedding order. A sovereign announcement
would not itself deliver power or water. Distribution operators would execute
service subject to network condition and available supply.

This nexus creates the domestic opportunity cost of export policy. Gas retained
for electricity cannot simultaneously feed LNG or reinjection. Crude burned for
summer generation cannot be exported or refined. Electricity denied to pumps,
processing, desalination, ports, or storage can reduce both domestic welfare and
future exportability. Water shortage can constrain labor, cooling, refining,
petrochemicals, or field operations. Conversely, demand restraint, fuel
switching, efficient desalination, stored water, or reserve margin can preserve
exports during a shock.

The industrial chain would also admit fertilizer and food inputs through the
common commodity grammar. Potash, phosphate rock, sulfur, gas-derived ammonia
and urea, transport, seasonal demand, and agricultural use would remain
separate where concentration or substitution changes food cost or availability.
These products test mining, rail and port dependence, seasonal inventories, and
slow substitution rather than extending an oil-specific response equation.

The minimum opening ledger would record technology-specific nameplate and
available capacity, fuel contracts and inventories, hourly or daily peak-load
shape at the simulation's supported resolution, water stocks by quality,
network losses, priority classes, administered prices, fiscal subsidies, and
outage or maintenance queues. Observations would distinguish operator truth
from delayed generation, fuel-burn, reservoir, and service-interruption reports.

The research baseline would use the World Bank's
[GCC water-energy nexus study](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/gcc/publication/advancing-knowledge-of-the-water-energy-nexus-in-the-gcc-countries)
and national utility or statistical sources. Country detail would be promoted
for a selected period only when a technology, allocation authority, or network
constraint changes petroleum exports, fiscal exposure, inflation, or visible
human consequences.
