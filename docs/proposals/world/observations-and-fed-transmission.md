# Gulf observations and Federal Reserve transmission proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.observations-and-fed-transmission`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.logistics-chokepoints`, `proposal.world.fiscal-monetary-portfolio-transmission`, `proposal.world.cross-era-middle-east-scenarios`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.economy.opening-conditions-and-transmission`, `design.interface.learning-and-run-knowledge`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

The player would encounter the Gulf through dated evidence, not a privileged
regional state panel. Every observation would name a publisher, subject,
coverage, method, unit, release time, reference time, vintage, revision policy,
uncertainty, and access path. Reports could disagree because they measure
different things or because one is wrong; disagreement would not alter the
underlying physical or accounting state.

Foreign developments would remain part of the continuous period information
environment before their economic importance was known. Newspapers,
broadcasts, wire reports, market commentary, diplomatic traffic, intelligence,
and staff assessments would differ persistently in access, selection,
interpretation, and incentives rather than carry generic reliability scores.
Reading delivered material would be free and the archive permanent. The player
could highlight, annotate, categorize, link, watch, and share evidence; casual
sharing could steer staff attention, while commissioned analysis or meetings
would consume institutional capacity.

The minimum evidence surface would include producer announcements and estimates;
field or terminal nominations where available; tanker position and passage
estimates; port queues; freight and charter rates; war-risk insurance terms;
crew or carrier notices; pipeline and terminal outage claims; commercial and
strategic inventories; refinery runs; crude-grade and location spreads; spot and
term LNG prices; futures curves and option-implied risk; sanctions and banking
notices; diplomatic statements; fiscal and reserve releases; market contacts;
and U.S. price, activity, credit, and expectations data.

Signals would map to hypotheses rather than conclusions. Falling Hormuz traffic
could mean physical closure, producer withholding, storage limits, insurer or
crew refusal, destination avoidance, reporting loss, or diversion. A price jump
could reflect expected durable supply loss, precautionary inventory demand,
freight and insurance cost, refinery mismatch, financial positioning, or an
inflation-risk premium. A production pledge could be infeasible, too slow,
wrong-grade, domestically absorbed, or unable to reach buyers.

Typed transmissions would carry consequences into existing domestic owners:

- delivered energy and import schedules affect firms and physical markets;
- freight capacity, delay, and insurance affect landed cost and availability;
- observed prices enter measurement and belief formation, not canonical truth;
- household and firm expectations affect wage, price, inventory, and spending
  decisions through their own information sets;
- foreign financial stress and dollar funding affect banks and markets; and
- foreign fiscal and portfolio responses affect demand, trade, and U.S. assets.

Scenario authors would define a hidden causal diagnosis and a researched set of
observable discriminants. Evaluation would reward correctly bounded claims,
updating across vintages, and policy matched to persistence and transmission.
It would not require omniscience or reward memorizing the historical outcome.

An optional regional dossier could organize public goals, staff hypotheses,
supporting and contradictory evidence, and analytical disagreement. It would
never expose the actor's canonical aspiration graph during play. The
retrospective could compare the hidden causal history, the evidence available at
each decision, official assessments, and the player's preserved annotations.

The Federal Reserve action surface would remain its lawful monetary, liquidity,
supervisory, communications, market-monitoring, and international-coordination
portfolio for the selected era. Advice or coordination could affect a foreign
channel only when an explicit feedback edge exists. Otherwise the player can
change U.S. resilience and interpretation, not the authored political path.
