# Gulf regional composition boundary proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.regional-composition-boundary`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.arabian-reference-chain`, `proposal.world.national-resource-management`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.content.regional-promotion`, `design.representation.coalitions-sovereigns-and-geography`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

The worked region would be a functional Gulf resource system, not a claim that
all included actors belong to the Arabian Peninsula. Its Arabian core would
contain Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain,
and Yemen. Iran and southern Iraq would enter as Gulf counterparties wherever
their production, terminals, sanctions, navigation, or conflict closes a
selected flow. Israel, Egypt, Turkey, and other powers would remain external
unless one attributable action changes a selected security, route, demand, or
financial channel.

Geographic scopes would overlap without owning behavior:

- Arabian Peninsula for land, jurisdiction, and producer membership;
- Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman for fields, terminals, storage, and navigation;
- Strait of Hormuz for passage capacity and security conditions;
- Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb for westbound Saudi exports and alternate routing;
- Indian Ocean buyer lanes for tanker and LNG destinations; and
- sovereign jurisdictions for rights, law, accounts, and public authority.

The research roster would begin with these candidate roles:

| Scope | Candidate independently varying roles |
|---|---|
| Saudi Arabia | oil and gas authority, Aramco operations, East-West route, refining and domestic burn, fiscal authority, SAMA, sovereign portfolios |
| United Arab Emirates | federal authority, Abu Dhabi resource owner, ADNOC operations, Fujairah route, central bank, sovereign portfolios |
| Qatar | QatarEnergy production and LNG, shipping commitments, fiscal authority, central bank, sovereign portfolios |
| Kuwait | petroleum authority and operator, refining, fiscal authority, central bank, sovereign portfolio |
| Oman | oil and gas operations, LNG, terminals outside Hormuz, fiscal and monetary authorities |
| Bahrain | refining, finance, domestic demand, and authorities only when independently material |
| Yemen | recorded security, port, and Bab el-Mandeb effects unless a later scenario promotes more |
| Iran | petroleum authority and operations, sanctions eligibility, settlement, storage, bounded security posture |
| Southern Iraq | federal authority, production, export allocation, gathering, and Gulf terminals |

The default boundary would promote only roles with independent choices, balance
sheets, constraints, or observations. A selected country could therefore split
resource ownership, operator execution, route allocation, fiscal claims,
monetary settlement, and portfolio behavior without simulating its whole state.
Internal emirate, court, factional, or regime politics would remain authored.
War, revolution, embargo, alliance, and regime transitions would follow
`doc:design.economy.external-provider-contracts`.

Every country would retain a lightweight sovereign record sufficient to own
territory, jurisdiction, institutions, resources, obligations, and external
relationships. Resolution would be unequal rather than representation being
optional. Durable institutions would retain capabilities and history across a
long campaign. Cabinets, revolutionary committees, strikes, and wartime
coalitions would appear only as temporary formations contesting control of
those institutions. A major succession could apply an authored inheritance
filter to commitments, grievances, and beliefs without invoking a general
regime or faction simulator.

Every promoted owner would replace an exact country-product-route-window share
of a residual Gulf provider. Unpromoted emirates, ministries, firms, fields,
buyers, carriers, and political factions would remain reconciled aggregates.
No `region.*` row would own reserves, production, policy, prices, inventories,
receipts, or military decisions.
