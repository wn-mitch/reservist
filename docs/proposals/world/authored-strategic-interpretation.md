# Authored strategic interpretation proposal

**ID:** `proposal.world.authored-strategic-interpretation`
**Status:** `proposal`
**Depends on:** `proposal.world.national-resource-management`, `proposal.world.cross-era-middle-east-scenarios`
**Proposes changes to:** `design.state.cognition-beliefs-and-plans`, `design.representation.fidelity-and-actor-behavior`, `design.economy.external-provider-contracts`

This proposal is nonbinding. It makes no catalog entry or scenario ready.

Foreign sovereigns would possess authored strategic interpretations and
endogenous operational behavior. Material conditions would establish pressures,
dependencies, capabilities, and feasible actions without determining a political
destiny. The simulation could surprise the author about whether a strategy
succeeds, but could not reveal that a sovereign secretly held an un-authored
theory of itself or the world.

A promoted sovereign would receive:

- three or four interior concerns describing what the ruling order must protect;
- three to six external commitments or relationships;
- beliefs about each counterparty's capability, willingness, compatibility of
  interests, domestic durability, and demonstrated reliability;
- two or three authored postures describing how the interior concerns are to be
  secured; and
- a finite repertoire of ordinary responses and authored escalation gates.

This is theory of mind, not a diplomatic opinion score. A sovereign
might judge a partner militarily capable but politically unwilling, or presently
willing but unable to sustain its promise. Confidence and ambiguity would remain
separate. Only beliefs capable of changing a selected
production, route, settlement, financial, or Fed-facing channel would be
instantiated.

The causal sequence would be:

`material position -> interior concerns -> beliefs about foreign commitments ->`
`strategic posture -> eligible actions -> execution and consequences`

Major wars, revolutions, alliance ruptures, embargoes, regimes, and new foreign
policy doctrines would remain authored exigencies under
`doc:design.economy.external-provider-contracts`. Within that boundary,
ministries, operators, central banks, sovereign funds, carriers, and other owners
could respond endogenously. Ordinary production changes, route diversification,
borrowing, reserve use, and portfolio rebalancing could be eligible. Deliberate
financial warfare, an oil embargo, or an interstate attack would require an
authored branch even when an ordinary transaction could mechanically resemble
coercion.

Belief revision would be event-driven. A typed commitment test would identify
the promisor, promise, stakes, observed response, material consequence,
ambiguity, and information available to the sovereign. The actor would interpret
the test from its authored priors and could revise beliefs within the supplied
posture vocabulary. A failed interception might weaken confidence in a security
guarantee and increase route diversification; random variation could not directly
make Saudi Arabia hostile to an ally.

Contingency would enter through uncertainty, material incidents, and game
inputs. These events could change evidence, capacity, cost, or outcomes rather
than rewrite interests. Player actions would affect foreign judgment only
through declared, reachable channels.

For Saudi Arabia, persistent concerns could include dynastic survival,
territorial security, domestic material stability, custodial legitimacy,
petroleum income, and strategic autonomy. Period content would author how the
House of Saud believed those interests were protected in 1979, 1990, or the
contemporary crisis. This would remain a bounded strategic event chain, not a
simulation of royal courts, factions, classes, or the complete international
system.

Sunni religious authority, Shia religious authority, and Arab-Palestinian
legitimacy would remain independent regional forces rather than attributes of a
single sovereign. They could emit authored claims, recognition, and
mobilization pressure but would not directly own production, unrest, or state
policy. A sovereign aspiration graph would likewise remain an authoring and
runtime structure. During play, media and staff could expose public goals,
competing interpretations, and evidence without revealing the canonical branch.

Political succession would preserve durable institutions and material
inheritance while applying an authored filter to commitments, grievances,
foreign-reliability beliefs, and strategic vocabulary. Commercial obligations
would persist unless repudiated; political commitments would require
reaffirmation. Temporary cabinets,
revolutionary groups, strikes, or wartime coalitions could contest institutional
control without becoming permanent national faction simulations.
